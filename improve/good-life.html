A Good Life

A practical picture of the life I want to build: healthy, loving, interesting, useful, secure, adventurous, peaceful, and enjoyable.

The purpose of these goals is not to optimize every part of life or to create a perfect future version of myself. The goal is to build a life that feels good from the inside while also being one I can respect and feel proud of.

Specific targets may change over time. The deeper directions should remain more stable. Numbers and milestones are useful when they support the life I want, but they are not the life itself.

Core principle: Build a life that is healthy, loving, interesting, useful, free, meaningful, and enjoyable — without maximizing one part of life by bankrupting the others.

Body

I want to feel healthy, energetic, strong, rested, and physically capable. I want my body to expand what I can do rather than increasingly limit me.

Direction

  • Maintain a healthy and sustainable body weight
  • Sleep enough to feel rested and function well
  • Maintain strength, endurance, mobility, and balance
  • Eat in a way that supports health without making food miserable
  • Take preventive medical and dental care seriously
  • Recover properly from training and injury
  • Maintain good hygiene, grooming, and appearance
  • Stay physically capable well into older age

Current aspirations

  • Maintain a weight around 190 pounds, assuming that remains healthy and sustainable
  • Average roughly 8 hours of sleep
  • Run 3 miles comfortably at about 8 mph
  • Eventually run a 5K in under 20:32
  • Compete in a 5K occasionally, perhaps quarterly
  • Build toward a 1,000-pound combined squat, bench press, and deadlift total
  • Compete in an amateur powerlifting meet at least once if training remains enjoyable and healthy
  • Maintain regular stretching, mobility, and outdoor activity

Love

I want a romantic life characterized by warmth, affection, mutual attraction, companionship, respect, intimacy, honesty, and genuine enjoyment of one another.

Direction

  • Have a relationship in which both people actively choose one another
  • Experience affection, touch, warmth, and emotional closeness
  • Have a mutually satisfying sexual relationship
  • Communicate openly about intimacy, needs, boundaries, and concerns
  • Feel respected rather than tolerated
  • Give respect rather than contempt, ridicule, control, or hostility
  • Preserve individuality within partnership
  • Be able to enjoy ordinary daily life together
  • Build a life that is already good enough that a relationship enhances it rather than rescues it

Current aspirations

  • Build or maintain a romantic relationship that is sustainable over the long term
  • Prioritize mutual affection, respect, warmth, playfulness, and sexual compatibility
  • Address relationship problems honestly rather than allowing resentment to accumulate indefinitely
  • Remain capable of standing on my own emotionally, socially, and financially

Friendship

I want meaningful relationships with people I enjoy, trust, admire, and care about. I do not want my social life to depend entirely on work or a romantic partner.

Direction

  • Maintain several close friendships
  • Initiate contact instead of always waiting for others
  • Schedule time together deliberately
  • Be someone friends can rely on
  • Celebrate other people's success
  • Ask for help when needed and provide help when I can
  • Maintain friendships across different stages and parts of life
  • Participate in groups, activities, and communities beyond work

Current aspirations

  • Maintain roughly 5–10 meaningful relationships
  • Regularly reach out to people instead of allowing relationships to drift
  • Have recurring social rituals such as dinners, walks, trips, activities, or shared hobbies
  • Make new friends while continuing to invest in old ones

Mastery

I want to remain intellectually alive, professionally capable, curious, and creative. Learning should continue throughout my life rather than ending when formal schooling does.

Direction

  • Develop genuine competence rather than merely collecting credentials
  • Continue learning difficult and interesting things
  • Remain curious about science, technology, history, philosophy, culture, and people
  • Become increasingly capable in work that matters to me
  • Create things rather than only consuming information
  • Teach or share what I know
  • Remain willing to be a beginner
  • Keep work important without allowing it to become my entire identity

Current aspirations

  • Complete the M.S. in Artificial Intelligence
  • Continue growing from infrastructure and cloud engineering into deeper AI capability
  • Build practical AI projects rather than learning only in theory
  • Continue increasing professional competence, autonomy, and compensation
  • Maintain strong long-term employability
  • Always have something I am learning simply because it interests me
  • Maintain some form of creative or technical output: writing, coding, teaching, building, or another project

Service

I want some of my time, knowledge, money, and ability to improve lives other than my own. A good life should include contribution, not only personal achievement.

Direction

  • Volunteer
  • Help people without always needing recognition
  • Mentor people when my experience could be useful
  • Teach and share knowledge
  • Be generous within reasonable limits
  • Support worthwhile people and causes
  • Leave people, places, and organizations somewhat better than I found them

Current aspirations

  • Volunteer at least quarterly
  • Preferably develop a monthly volunteering habit
  • Look for opportunities to mentor or help people earlier in their careers or education
  • Use technical knowledge and life experience in ways that are useful to others

Adventure

I want life to contain novelty, travel, exploration, stories, laughter, awe, and experiences that exist for reasons other than productivity.

Direction

  • Travel regularly
  • Visit unfamiliar places
  • Spend time outdoors
  • Experience nature and awe
  • Try activities I have never done before
  • Keep hobbies and interests that are simply fun
  • Laugh often
  • Allow room for spontaneity
  • Occasionally choose the interesting option rather than the most efficient one
  • Always have something on the horizon to look forward to

Current aspirations

  • Take at least one meaningful trip or adventure each year
  • Continue traveling internationally, including returning to Japan
  • Spend time outdoors every week when practical
  • Keep entertainment, humor, books, movies, music, and hobbies in life without needing to justify them
  • Try occasional new experiences that are outside my normal routine
  • Maintain upcoming events, trips, races, dinners, or activities I can genuinely look forward to

Peace

I want ordinary life to feel stable, calm, secure, organized, and spacious enough to enjoy. I want enough money, time, autonomy, and emotional steadiness that life does not constantly feel like an emergency.

Direction

  • Maintain a clean, comfortable, functional home
  • Keep finances organized and increasingly secure
  • Build enough financial independence to have real choices
  • Maintain routines without becoming rigid
  • Protect sleep and downtime
  • Leave some time unscheduled
  • Develop greater emotional calm and confidence
  • Reduce unnecessary chaos
  • Avoid allowing work, school, relationships, or goals to consume every available hour
  • Learn when I already have enough
  • Enjoy ordinary days rather than living only for future milestones

Current aspirations

  • Keep my home consistently clean and reasonably organized
  • Maintain good personal hygiene and appearance
  • Eliminate unnecessary debt
  • Continue saving and investing consistently
  • Maintain a strong emergency fund
  • Increase financial independence and career optionality over time
  • Develop calmer reactions to uncertainty, conflict, and setbacks
  • Protect enough free time that life does not become a sequence of obligations
  • Have ordinary weeks that are pleasant, not merely productive

What Balance Means

The goal is not to maximize every category. A good life is more likely to come from having enough of several important things at the same time.

  • Do not destroy health in pursuit of career success
  • Do not sacrifice sleep to make room for every possible goal
  • Do not sacrifice friendships indefinitely for work or school
  • Do not sacrifice independence merely to preserve a relationship
  • Do not sacrifice financial security for appearances or status
  • Do not sacrifice enjoyment for constant self-optimization
  • Do not allow fitness goals to become more important than long-term health
  • Do not spend the present entirely preparing for a future that never arrives
Never maximize one area by bankrupting the others.

A Picture of the Life

I want to be healthy, strong, energetic, and well-rested; calm and comfortable in my own skin; financially secure enough to have meaningful choices; engaged in challenging work and lifelong learning; surrounded by people I genuinely care about; part of a warm, affectionate, and sexually fulfilling partnership; useful to other people; regularly exploring, traveling, laughing, creating, and spending time outdoors; and free enough to enjoy ordinary days instead of constantly racing toward the next achievement.

I want to reach older age feeling that I used my abilities well, loved people well, learned continuously, helped where I could, experienced a lot, and actually enjoyed the life I spent so much time building.

The Seven Areas

  • Body — health, sleep, strength, endurance, capability
  • Love — intimacy, affection, partnership, sexuality
  • Friendship — belonging, connection, community
  • Mastery — career, learning, creation, competence
  • Service — contribution, volunteering, mentoring, generosity
  • Adventure — novelty, travel, play, nature, awe
  • Peace — calm, autonomy, security, time, enough

Specific goals should serve these areas rather than becoming ends in themselves. If a goal stops improving the life described here, it can be changed or discarded.
