Current Goals

The small number of things I am deliberately working to improve right now.

My Growth Goals describe the kind of person I want to become. My Good Life describes the life I want to build. This page answers a much simpler question:

What deserves my deliberate attention right now?

I do not need to actively improve every part of life at the same time. Most good things can remain in maintenance while a few priorities receive focused effort. When one area becomes reasonably stable, I can move another goal into active development.

The aim is not a perfect week. The aim is to make important behaviors increasingly normal, repeatable, and sustainable.

Current Focus

Keep the active list short. Three major areas is usually enough.

1. Sleep & Recovery

Why: Sleep supports mood, judgment, learning, emotional regulation, training, health, and nearly every other goal on this page.

Current target:

  • Aim for roughly 8 hours of sleep per night
  • Use approximately 9:00 PM to 5:00 AM as the default sleep window when practical
  • Begin winding down before bedtime rather than treating bedtime as the point when activity stops
  • Avoid routinely borrowing sleep to create more time for work, school, entertainment, or exercise
  • Notice patterns that interfere with sleep and correct them rather than accepting chronic sleep deprivation

Success looks like:

  • Most nights provide enough sleep
  • Morning fatigue is decreasing
  • The schedule feels increasingly automatic rather than requiring constant effort

2. Health, Weight & Physical Capability

Why: I want to become leaner, stronger, faster, and more physically capable while protecting long-term health and making exercise sustainable.

Current direction:

  • Continue moving toward approximately 190 pounds at a sustainable pace
  • Eat in a way that supports fat loss, health, training, and long-term maintenance
  • Run consistently and gradually improve endurance and speed
  • Continue strength training with emphasis on squat, bench press, and deadlift
  • Include mobility, stretching, walking, and recovery
  • Avoid allowing short-term performance goals to override injury prevention or recovery

Longer-term direction:

  • Run 3 miles comfortably at about 8 mph
  • Eventually run a 5K in under 20:32
  • Participate in occasional 5K races
  • Build toward a 1,000-pound combined squat, bench press, and deadlift total
  • Compete in an amateur powerlifting meet if training remains healthy and enjoyable

Success right now looks like:

  • Following the process more often than not
  • Weight trending gradually in the desired direction
  • Running fitness improving
  • Strength improving without accumulating unnecessary injuries

3. Relationships, Friendship & Social Connection

Why: A good life requires people. Relationships are unlikely to remain strong through good intentions alone; they need time, attention, initiation, and shared experiences.

Current target:

  • Maintain existing close friendships instead of allowing them to drift
  • Initiate contact with at least two people each week
  • Try to schedule at least one meaningful social activity every week or two
  • Remain open to making new friends
  • Express appreciation instead of assuming people know they matter
  • Invest in warmth, respect, communication, affection, and honesty in close relationships
  • Maintain friendships and interests outside a romantic relationship

Success looks like:

  • I am regularly talking with people I care about
  • I have upcoming social plans rather than waiting for spontaneous connection
  • Important relationships feel active rather than dormant
  • I am both giving and receiving support

Weekly Review

Once a week, spend about 15–20 minutes reviewing the system. Do not turn this into a large planning exercise.

1. How is the overall life?

  • Body: Am I taking care of my health, sleep, fitness, and recovery?
  • Love: Is there warmth, honesty, respect, intimacy, and connection?
  • Friendship: Am I maintaining meaningful relationships?
  • Mastery: Am I learning, working, and creating?
  • Service: Am I useful to people beyond myself?
  • Adventure: Am I exploring, playing, and experiencing novelty?
  • Peace: Does ordinary life feel reasonably calm, secure, and manageable?

2. What went well this week?

  • What should I keep doing?
  • What am I proud of?
  • What became easier?

3. What is drifting?

  • Is sleep slipping?
  • Am I avoiding exercise or overeating?
  • Have I stopped contacting people?
  • Is work or school consuming too much of life?
  • Is the house becoming chaotic?
  • Am I spending all my free time distracted instead of actually enjoying myself?

4. What are the most important actions next week?

Choose a few concrete actions rather than creating a giant task list.

  • One action for health
  • One action for relationships
  • One important work or school action
  • One thing I am looking forward to

5. Is one goal harming another part of life?

  • Am I sacrificing sleep for productivity?
  • Am I sacrificing relationships for work?
  • Am I sacrificing recovery for fitness?
  • Am I sacrificing enjoyment for optimization?
  • Am I adding goals when I actually need more margin?

When to Change the Active Goals

Do not change priorities merely because something new feels exciting. Give important changes enough time to become established.

  • Use roughly 8–12 week focus periods when practical
  • Keep a goal active until the behavior is reasonably stable or the goal is no longer useful
  • Move stable behaviors into maintenance
  • Add a new active goal only when there is enough capacity for it
  • Change goals when evidence or circumstances change
  • Abandon goals that no longer support the life I actually want
The purpose of the system is not to constantly feel behind.

Work deliberately on a few things. Maintain the rest. Enjoy life while doing it.

The Hierarchy

When I feel overwhelmed by goals, return to this:

  • Growth Goals: Who am I trying to become?
  • Good Life: What kind of life am I trying to build?
  • Current Goals: What deserves deliberate attention now?
  • This Week: What are the next few actions?
  • Today: What is the next useful thing to do?
Values guide the direction.
Vision describes the destination.
Goals choose the current priorities.
Habits and actions create the life.
